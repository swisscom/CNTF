    Parallel sessions on cloud-native topics.

    14:00–14:50

    • OpenMind: The Integration Tax: An Independent Supplier’s Perspective on Building a Pathway to CNF

      Details

      Operators are moving to cloud-native network functions at very different speeds, and from very different starting points. Drawing on work with operators of every tier worldwide, Openmind Networks looks at why these journeys have become so fragmented and what operators can do now to prepare the ground, so that vendors can deliver CNF in their networks effectively. The session also covers the organisational side of the shift: the changes in skills, structures and ways of working needed to support the technical move, which are often the harder part.

    • Telefónica GitOps Journey:

      Details

      From Vendor Artifact Delivery to IMS deployment in AWS. A fully automated, event-driven GitOps pipeline

    • Telstra: Scaling cloud-native operations across Mobile Core

      Details

      Telstra's journey towards onboarding and managing CNFs at scale across Australia, using GitOps, declarative automation and standardised lifecycle/configuration management. The discussion will cover the foundations needed to operate this model at telco scale, the challenges that remain, and where greater operator and vendor alignment is required.

    • TDC: TDC NET Core Network Transformation & Automation Strategy

      Details

      I plan to walk the audience through TDC NET’s core network transformation journey, our current challenges related to lifecycle management (LCM) and observability, and our strategy and vision for automating core network operations.

      I look forward to sharing our experiences and exchanging ideas with the audience.

    15:00–15:50

    • Mavenir: Evolving to Autonomous Operations with GitOps and Cloud-Native CNFs

      Details

      Discover how GitOps and Cloud-native Network Functions (CNFs) provide the foundation for autonomous network operations. This session examines how infrastructure and application lifecycle automation, CI/CD practices, observability, and AI-driven insights work together to improve agility, consistency, and operational efficiency. Learn how communications service providers (CSPs) can reduce manual processes, accelerate service delivery, workload migration from VNFs to CNFs, and build a scalable path toward self-managing networks.

    • ATT: Deterministic ISSU through Atomicity

      Details

      ATT shares their autonomous lifecycle management solution for the cloud native 5G Core. In this, ATT like to get input and alignment on decomposed, independently deployable helm charts and the use of the atomic helm operation and helm tests to ensure upgrades are performed in a safe, atomic manner at the NF level, ending either at the target release or the starting release without operator intervention.

      This is in the vein of the software freshness work stream, and I’ll share our views on ISSU/R/D.

    • Deutsche Telekom: OCAS is operator based unified life cycle automation platform for cloud-native telco services.

    • Ericsson: CSP–Vendor Continuous Feedback Loop

      Details

      How can CSPs and vendors create a continuous feedback loop that benefits both sides? What information should be shared, what makes sharing it across company boundaries difficult, and what practical approaches could help? Join the discussion and share your perspective.

Agentic AI in Telco/TechCo

Thursday, 26 November · Bern · CET

  1. 08:30–09:00Check-in, Welcome Coffee ☕

    Keynote with MCP Gateway Demo by Swisscom

  3. 10:00–12:20

    Breakout Sessions Day 2 · Four parallel sessions per round

    Parallel sessions on agentic AI in Telco/TechCo.

    10:30–11:20

    • OpenMind: From Hype to Reality: Practical AI Operations and Software Delivery in Telco Network

      Details

      Examining real-world AI Ops use cases requested by MNOs globally, exploring how AI is embedded across the software development lifecycle, and sharing practical approaches for leveraging AI in automated testing to drive autonomous network operations.

    • TNO: agentic AI tool for automated migration of cloud‑native applications

      Details

      Although Kubernetes originally aimed to enable seamless portability across cloud providers, the widespread use of Custom Resources for networking, storage, and policy control has introduced practical incompatibilities. At TNO, we are developing a proof‑of‑concept tool that automatically translates configuration files between incompatible Kubernetes environments, significantly reducing the effort required to migrate applications, particularly for workloads with advanced network policy requirements. The tool integrates with standardized CAMARA APIs at the network layer, supporting deployment and operation of network‑aware applications across heterogeneous cloud infrastructures.

    • Orange, Vodafone & Swisscom: Collaborative AI for Network Operations—Shared Skills, Smarter Troubleshooting at Sylva Hackathon

      Details

      Telco-specific AI agents and AI-driven root cause analysis are promising areas of innovation, but no operator needs to solve the challenges alone. Drawing on work from the Sylva Hackathon, Orange and Swisscom will explore how reusable agent skills could help AI understand network operations, correlate alarms with metrics, logs and traces, and support troubleshooting. Join the discussion on how operators can work together on the research, shared foundations and safeguards needed to bring these initiatives closer to operational use.

    • Oracle: Agentic AI for Network AIOps: From Insight to Action

      Details

      As telecom networks become more complex, operations teams face growing pressure to maintain service quality, resolve incidents faster and improve efficiency. AI can help detect anomalies and surface insights—but the next opportunity is to connect those insights with timely, coordinated action.

      This session presents Oracle’s perspective on how agentic AI can advance Network AIOps. By combining network telemetry, operational knowledge and service context, AI agents can help teams investigate problems, assess their impact and coordinate responses across operational tools and workflows.

      Practical use cases illustrate the approach, including detecting degradation and prioritizing intervention based on customer and business impact; and supporting remediation through recommended actions, human approval and controlled execution, followed by verification of the outcome.

      The session explores the foundations needed to make these capabilities dependable: trusted data, integration across multiple environments, clear operational guardrails and traceable decisions. It offers a practical path towards greater network autonomy.

    11:30–12:20

    • Mavenir: Who’s on the Call? Agentic AI and Differentiated Connectivity

      Details

      Much of today's AI-for-networks discussion focuses on automation and operational efficiency, but a bigger question is emerging: how will AI change the services and experiences networks deliver? As AI agents begin to initiate and participate in communications, and AI capabilities enhance and adapt live sessions, operators face a strategic choice about which aspects of the experience they should enable, control, or expose to external platforms. This session will explore the implications for differentiated connectivity, trust, security, quality, sovereignty, and open interfaces, and where the industry needs to align.

    • Ericsson: The Path to Robust Agentic AI

      Details

      Will we ever be able to use agents to manage society critical services? Can we afford not to? Where are you focusing agentic efforts initially and how are you mitigating risks?

    • AWS: Agentic Network Operation Benchmarks

      Details

      AI agents are starting to act on networks, not only advise on them. Before operators give an agent write access, they need evidence of how it behaves on real operational tasks: whether it reaches the intended state, stays within its permissions and leaves the system clean. This session presents early, exploratory work on a benchmark that evaluates agents on network operation tasks. We will share why we started it, how it is designed and what we have learned so far. We welcome feedback and ideas from the community on which tasks, safety checks and evidence would make such a benchmark useful, and are interested in hearing from those working on similar questions.

    • Telstra: Agentic AI: From AI-assisted operations towards autonomous networks

      Details

      How Telstra's approaching Agentic AI across Mobile Core, including practical operational use cases, ANLET maturity targets and the path towards greater closed-loop autonomy.
